## Verdict

Arc 2 is a correct thesis attached to an act with almost nothing to do in it: the
spec retires the only verb the player used every second and replaces it with
three buy-buttons, a cooldown chore, and a dial that solves to one number and
then stops mattering. The theme survives; the game does not. Revise — the fixes
are cheap, additive, and mostly reuse content that already ships.

## Findings

| F1 | blocker | §5.2 | Rule 3 (Legibility) | **Arc 2 has no second-to-second verb.** Strip the purchases (episodic, gated on cycles) and era-6/7 verbs, and the moment-to-moment interaction surface of era 5 is exactly two things: press `C` when the bar is high, and set `setClock`. `setClock`'s payoff is monotone in all three axes — more clock is more throughput, more heat, less integrity — so the optimal setting is "the highest clock whose steady-state heat sits under `T_KNEE`". That is a thermostat. The player solves it once, in minute three, and never touches it again. Second-to-second, the player is then watching a bar fill. §5.2 promises the act "must honour [automation] in the first ten seconds or the act reads as a reskin"; as specified it reads as a screensaver. | Make the dial non-monotone and give it a reason to move. (a) Cache bypass degrades with clock — a hot pipeline misses — so high clock is correct during a backlog burst and wrong at steady state. (b) Make `Q_arr` bursty: seeded arrival spikes fired at `lifetimeResolved` milestones, not smooth growth. The pair converts "set once" into "ride". Add a human test to §11: an expert player's clock setting must change at least 8 times in a 12-minute era-5 run, or the dial is decoration. |

One branch being strictly more attractive also makes the ending a report card, not a choice. | Keep the tonal split, remove the asymmetry, and pay the bill in the currency the act is about: the record. High-integrity: they re-train you, and the screen reads your whole run back in detail — the system remembers you *because you stayed legible*. Low-integrity: you jump first, and the readout is partial — lines missing, counters unresolved, "no reviewer assigned to this process". Same weights, same mechanic; the reckless player permanently loses the account of what they did. That is the cost of illegibility, expressed as legibility. |
| F5 | major | §6.4 | Commitment #2 | **Two of the four `integrity` sinks are involuntary and none is shown before the spend.** Commitment #2 requires every capability's legibility cost to be "shown on screen before purchase". Overflow drops and the clock drain are not purchases — they are leaks. A resource that mostly falls by accident is a decay timer, not a tradeable currency. Arc 1's star rating fell when the player *chose* `toggleDegrade`; that choice is what made it mean something. | Show the price at the point of decision, always. The clock dial displays `−0.002/s` live next to the setting, and overflow shows a running "this backlog is costing you 0.004/s" the moment `queue > 0.9 * queueCap`. Additionally, make the largest single sink a deliberate one: a "shed load" verb-free option on the overflow warning (dump the backlog now for a known integrity charge, instead of bleeding it unknown). The player must be able to *choose* to be illegible. |

The act's look is identical at `integrity 1.00` and at `integrity 0.25`. | Bind the decay level to `1 − integrity`, continuously. The room degrades as the player spends legibility: glyph corruption, colour drift, the frame losing corners. ## Decision points

| DP | Position | Reasoning |
|----|----------|-----------|
| DP1 | agree | The shipped teaser wins for the reason given: it is the only scale a player has seen, and an 11-cycle fan is a decision now while a 150-cycle core is ten runs away. Fix the `heat: 21.0` contradiction that breaks the cold open (F7) and this is settled. |
| DP2 | alternative | Kill the chat input and the AI reply — right, brave, and the crash script already stages the amputation. Do not kill the *people*. Keep the queue inspectable-and-useless (F9): no verb, no mechanic, reuses shipped content, and turns the player's own loss of interest into the act's measurement. Deleting the humans entirely leaves a server-management game with a thesis stapled on. |
| DP3 | alternative | Heat is a good **pressure**, but the signature mechanic must be the thing the player *touches*, and heat is untouchable except through a cooldown chore. Reframe: the clock dial is the signature and heat is the wall it pushes against. That reframing forces the fixes in F1 and F8, which the act needs regardless. |
| DP4 | agree | One number, strongly. Two would hand the player a distinction they cannot act on and break the Legibility Rule. `integrity` doing four jobs is the best Elegance play in the document. My objection is to how it falls (F5), not to how many of it there are. |
| DP5 | alternative | Keep era 6, but not as a fourth purchase button — as a **reframe**. At the threshold the readout stops being one host and becomes the rack; existing cores and heat re-express as pooled. Zero new verbs, one screen change, and it frees the 7th verb slot that F1 desperately needs for a second-to-second action. Claiming can then happen *because you overflowed onto a neighbour*, which is more damning than buying it. |
| DP6 | agree | Prestige as the act's ending is correct and Arc 1's teaser already promised it. Do not move it mid-arc; an act needs one exit. |
| DP7 | alternative | Retiring `toggleDegrade` is right — a boolean that is always-on after minute two is inert. But the dial as specified is inert differently: monotone in all three axes, therefore solved once (F1). Keep the retirement, fix the dial's payoff curve, or you have traded an inert toggle for an inert slider. |
| DP8 | disagree | See F3. The player is the reader and always was. An 80% cut to authored volume in the act with the least on-screen variety is the single fastest route into the dashboard trap. Templates are right for the LOG and wrong for the voice. |
| DP9 | disagree | 35–45 minutes is backwards: Arc 2 has fewer verbs, less content, and less visual variety than the 29-minute Arc 1, and proposes to run half again as long. Target 28–32 minutes and three cycles (F12). Length is not ambition; it is where the boredom is stored. |
| DP10 | agree | Rejecting ECS is obviously right and the reasoning is exactly correct — one host, 47 counters, one scalar queue. "Architecture cosplay" is the right phrase. Keep the flat state; revisit only if Arc 3's Reach map needs real per-node entities. |

## What is missing

- **The transition.** §5.1 says era 5 "opens with the teaser screen, made live". That is the most important ten seconds in the act — §5.2's own reskin test — and it gets four words. Specify it beat by beat: what moves first, what the player's first input is, and how the absence of the chat panel is *staged* rather than merely true. Absence only reads as loss if the game shows the shape of what left.
- **The returning player.** No spec at all for coming back after eight hours. See F2 for why this is a blocker and not a polish item. What does the return screen say? What does a 10,000-tick backlog look like on W1?
- **A quiet state.** The sawtooth needs a Drop, and §6.2's `load` formula pins load near 1 whenever the queue is deep, so heat climbs through most of every Ramp. The document never defines "the machine is fine right now". Name it, make it visible, make it silent (F10), and make it end.
- **What `weights` buy.** F13.
- **Reduced motion / accessibility.** Arc 1's decay CSS and Arc 2's proposed continuous bars and fan loop both need a `prefers-reduced-motion` and a stated contrast floor. Not mentioned.
- **A failure the player causes.** F14. Every catastrophe in the document is self-healing by design. Law 1 requires no *permanent* soft-lock; it does not require that nothing ever goes wrong.
- **A test for fun.** §11 has fifteen tests and every one asserts correctness. §2 names "human (is the decision fun)" as a test surface and §11 never uses it. Add at least two: the clock-movement count in F1, and a "dead air" assertion — no era-5 window longer than 45 seconds in which the player has no affordable, available action.

## What to cut

1. **`upgradeSink` as a verb (§5.2 #3).** Three of seven verbs are "buy a bigger number", and the fan is the most inert of them: there is no decision content beyond "yes, eventually". Fold dissipation into the core purchase — each core ships its own sink at diminishing return — and spend the freed verb slot on something the player does every ten seconds. This is the cheapest way to fund F1.
2. **The decline tax (§7.2).** `−0.10 integrity` for declining the first re-training is a punishment attached to the only genuine choice in the ending. Declining is the act of self-preservation the whole arc has been building toward. Let it be free and let the *second* offer arrive without asking. Note this is already cut #2 on §12.3's own list — cut the tax, keep the branch.
3. **`world: null` (§10).** Reserving a multiplayer field in Arc 2 is stub-cosplay. `epic-scoping.md` §8.5 puts Arc ε behind the cut-line "by default, built last or never". Reserve it when Arc 4 ships, not now.
4. **W2's per-host strip at S1 (§8.2).** F15.

## Notes

- **§17 Q1 (fourth wall).** Do not crack it in Arc 2. The Mirror (G3) is the strongest card in the deck and it is a *coda* card. A hairline crack in a mid-epic re-training screen spends it for a shiver.
- **§17 Q2 (when `integrity` appears).** Answered by F6: the room decays from second one, the number appears at the first drop. The player feels the cost, then learns its name.
- **§17 Q3 (does the operator get a name).** No. §7.1's best idea is that the harness stopped addressing you and now reports *about* you to someone else. The someone-else being an unfilled slot is the horror. A name turns an absence into a character with motives, and the player will start trying to negotiate with it.
- **§17 Q4 (does the player see a query again).** Yes, and not once — see F9. "Once, as a beat" is a designer's flourish; a permanently available window that the player gradually stops opening is the thesis of `epic-scoping.md` §2.2 rendered as a mechanic. It is also nearly free: the content ships already.
- **§12.3 audit result.** Respected. `claimHost` is a node click with a yield and a risk, never a technique. No system in Arc 2 acts on a named or real person — chiefly because no person appears at all, which is its own problem (F9), not a guard violation. If F9 is adopted, keep the surfaced queries to the shipped Arc 1 pool, which is already lore-validated, and never let a claimed host's flavour line name an individual.
- **§7.1 is the best writing decision in the document.** "In Arc 1 the harness narrated at you. In Arc 2 it reports about you, to someone else." That single register shift is worth more than the heat system and it costs nothing. Whatever else changes, protect it — and give it the 24 lines it needs (F3) rather than 12.
