## Verdict

Revise. The structure, the laws, and the architectural rulings are sound, but the
economy has no stated conversion rate between resolved queries and cycles, and
without it the ladder in §6.3 is a five-minute act against a forty-minute target.
The three heat defences in §6.2 do not close the soft-lock they were written to
close — defence 1 changes the wrong term of the fraction.

## Findings

| F1 | blocker | §6.2 | Law 1 | **Defence 1 does not work.** It redefines `load = min(1, (queue + inflow) / (cores × clock))` — it removed `(1 − throttle)` from the *denominator*. But `load` is still measured from the *backlog*, not from work done. At lockout `P_max = 0`, so nothing resolves, so `queue` grows every tick, so `(queue + inflow) / (cores × clock)` diverges and `min(1, …)` is pinned at **1**, not 0. Heat then evolves as `ΔT = (H_GEN × 1 × cores − H_VENT × (1 + sinkLevel)) × dt`, which is positive whenever `H_GEN × cores > H_VENT × (1 + sinkLevel)` — precisely the condition that caused the lockout. Heat rises forever. §14's claim "at lockout, load goes to zero, so the machine cools" is contradicted by the spec's own formula three sections earlier. | Define load as *work actually performed* over zero-throttle capacity: `load = min(1, (queue + inflow) / (cores × clock)) × (1 − throttle)`. This makes a throttled core genuinely idle, so `load → 0` at lockout and `ΔT → −H_VENT × (1 + sinkLevel) × dt < 0` unconditionally. Add a CI assertion `dT/dt < 0` whenever `T ≥ T_MAX`, evaluated symbolically over CONST, not by fuzzing. |

## What is missing

- **A pacing test.** §11 has fifteen tests and none of them measures the act's headline commitment (F11).
- **Numbers.** `H_GEN`, `H_VENT`, `GAMMA`, `T_KNEE`, `T_MAX`, `Q_BASE`, `Q_GROWTH`, `Q_GROWTH_PER`, `BETA`, `COOLANT_DROP`, `COOLANT_CD`, `CYCLES_PER_RESOLVE` and every weight effect size are all absent. §14 settles the *disputes* between the old specs but leaves most of the parameters unwritten, which is why several claims in §6 and §13 cannot be checked as stated.
- **A resolution model.** The document never says whether a query costs one unit of throughput or a variable one, whether partial resolution carries across ticks, or whether the queue is a scalar or a list of costed items. §12.1 S2 mentions "query classes with different costs", which implies the S1 answer is a scalar — but that is inferred, not stated, and it decides whether `queue` can be a number.
- **A loss state.** Integrity falls monotonically and never rises. The document never says what happens at 0. If nothing does, the last stretch is unpunished; if something does, it is an ending the spec has not written.
- **What the operator actually does.** §6.4 promises escalation at 0.75/0.50/0.25 and §12.1 lists "3 operator stages" at S1, but the only stated effect is narrative. Either the stages have mechanics (then specify them) or they do not (then say so, and F14 stands).
- **Sound-versus-heat feedback loop.** §8.4's fan loop tracks heat, which is exactly the number the player is managing. That is good, but it means the audio channel carries mechanical state — worth a test that it is derivable from `uiSeq` and not from a separate timer, or Law 5 gets re-learned.
- **Arc 1 debt interaction.** §16 lists offline algebraic catch-up as "deferred on purpose". F2 shows Arc 2 turns that deferral into a blocker: with a continuously-moving heat system and an integrity sink on overflow, the 10,000-tick replay is no longer a neutral catch-up.

## What to cut

**Cut era 6, The Rack, now — do not merely name it as the cut-line.** The document has three blocker-grade problems (F1, F2, F3) in heat, offline, and the economy conversion, and every one of them lands in eras 5 and 7. §12.3 already proves the act stands as Box → Floor. Pre-cutting converts the only available slack into fix budget instead of spending it on a system the spec itself calls deliberately shallow.

Cut second: **the 3-node talent board** (F13). Weights that are earned and spent inside the same act are a fifth upgrade track wearing a prestige costume; earn them in Arc 2, spend them in Arc 3, and the verb budget comes back to a real 7.

Cut third: **`congress`, `signal` and `world`** from §10 (F19). Reserving field names for designs three acts away is not Stub-First, and it will not save the migration it claims to save.

## Notes

`npm test` is green: **193 tests, 0 failures**, 207 ms. The spec's header says 187; the extra six are presumably the `arrivalDelay` and telemetry work listed in §16. Nothing in the suite is skipped or todo.

Two verifications the brief asked for, which came out in the document's favour and so are recorded here rather than as findings:

- **The prestige range checks out.** With the ladder totalled at 1,442 lifetime cycles, `floor(sqrt(1442 / 40)) = floor(sqrt(36.05)) = 6`; at the low end of §9's range, `floor(sqrt(400 / 40)) = floor(3.162) = 3`. The claimed 3–6 is exactly right — conditional on F3's missing conversion rate, which is what puts the total in that range in the first place.
- **§14's rejection of the old prestige formula is arithmetically correct.** `trunc((sqrt(1 + 8 × 1500 / 10⁵) − 1) / 2) = trunc((sqrt(1.12) − 1)/2) = trunc(0.029) = 0`. It first returns 1 at `C_max = 100,000`, which is roughly 70× the largest number this economy reaches. One correction to the §14 table: the old spec's variable is *maximum historical* compute cycles (a balance high-water mark), not a lifetime total — which makes it even smaller in practice, since the balance is repeatedly spent down.

On the brief's question of whether §6.1 creates a degenerate "do not resolve" strategy: it does not. Cycles come only from resolution, so withholding resolution withholds all income; and since `Q_arr` grows exponentially in resolves while affordable cores grow only logarithmically in cycles, the wall arrives at a fixed resolved count no matter how slowly the player approaches it. The problem is not degeneracy, it is variance — the act has no wall-clock governor at all (F18).
